# Thesauros for Tangem: $10,000,000 USDC, one year, backtest

Written on 11 October 2026. The live contracts were read on 10 October 2026, at Base block 52400764 and Ethereum block 26158339.

This pack is a backtest of one deposit of $10,000,000 USDC, made on 7 October 2025 and held to 7 October 2026, once on Base and once on Ethereum. A decision rule moves the deposit between lending venues. The history is on-chain readings about every four hours, 2,191 on Base and 2,180 on Ethereum.

**The result, for the main run of each network** (the first table below has every figure and its source):

- Left in Aave V3 and never moved, the deposit made 3.27% on Base and 3.51% on Ethereum.
- Under the allocator, at a fee of 0%, which is what both live vaults read on 10 October 2026, it made 4.60% and 4.08%: $133,407 and $57,425 more than in Aave.
- At the cap the contract allows, a fee of 20% on Base and 25% on Ethereum, which is the fee the runs were made at, it made 3.67% and 3.05%: $39,690 more than in Aave on Base and $46,128 less on Ethereum. The depositor equals Aave at a fee of 28.5% on Base and 13.8% on Ethereum.
- At a fee of 10% or more the depositor has less, on both networks, than the same venues held in equal parts with no rebalancer and no fee, which made 4.14% and 3.68%.
- On Base 96.2% of the vault stood in one Morpho market on average, directly and through vaults that lend into it. On Ethereum 31.1% and 27.9% of the interest came from Fluid fUSDC and Spark Savings sUSDC, two venues admitted without the rule's collateral test.

Three things to know before the tables.

- **It is not a track record.** Both live Thesauros USDC vaults were deployed inside the window: the Base vault (proxy `0x3C7739173cca612B6394EE57131458185A5beC44`) on 5 August 2026 (block 49576041), the Ethereum vault (proxy `0xc3156Da39EeEa9De80F1d74b497C0E4A7030Aae3`) on 21 September 2026 (block 26026159). On 10 October 2026 the Base vault held $1.01 and the Ethereum vault $1.00. The Base vault has never held more than $11.23; its rebalancer sent ten rebalance transactions between 14 August and 7 September 2026, the largest movement $6.72, and none from then to 10 October 2026. The Ethereum vault has had no deposit, withdrawal or rebalance since its $1 seed: the address that holds its executor role has never sent a transaction on Ethereum, and the Thesauros repository marks that vault read-only. So the public vault's rules, which two runs of this pack apply on Ethereum, have never moved money in that vault. Two older vaults with the same name and symbol are still on-chain: on Base `0xCA6029f2d5f5d36DA047206Ae7A254Fa80F579B8`, deployed on 11 February 2026, which held $17.94 on 10 October 2026 and has a 20% performance fee and a 1.5% management fee set, and on Ethereum `0x839E57080C18195D8D343a02c2f623b5916f7383`, which held $9.22. They are not the vaults of this pack. Nothing in this pack is a track record.
- **The rule simulated does not move the public vaults.** The main runs use the water-filling allocator of the Thesauros cross-chain planner. It is in the production repository. It is not the rule that moves the public vaults, and by the team's note of 8 October 2026 no production service was running it: its only deployment is a test stand of a few dollars. The planner acts on the contracts of the Thesauros cross-chain stack, another generation of contracts than the two public vaults, and its registry has Base and Arbitrum and no Ethereum network. The fee, the caps and the roles described in this readme are those of the public vault contract; the fee terms of the cross-chain stack were not read for this pack. The runs use a port of it, `decideWaterFill` of `reproduce/src/policies.ts`. Every decision of the Thesauros run of every allocator config, stress scenarios aside, was put to the production function `PlannerService.intraChainRebalance`: 34,941 decisions in sixteen runs, none differs (`allocator-check.txt`). That shows the port decides as that function does on the inputs the simulator built. It does not show that a running service would build the same inputs, and in every allocator run the function is fed through three rules that are the simulation's and not production's: the venue set by month, the size limit and the rule of no entry where liquidity is short. Two more of its inputs are choices of the simulation: the limit of 60% of the vault in a venue, where production reads each venue's limit from the strategy contract and those limits were not read, and the impact exponent of 1.75 for the venues production has no adapter for, among them the largest holding of the main run on Base and the two largest on Ethereum. No run of the pack shows the allocator without them. What else the comparison leaves out is in Method.
- **Most venues of the main runs are not providers of the live vaults.** Of the 7 venues of the main run on Base, 2 are providers of the live vault, 3 are Morpho vaults that the `MorphoProvider` contract of the live vaults reads but for which no provider is deployed, and 2 need a new adapter: Steakhouse Prime USDC V2 and the Morpho cbBTC market, which held 52.3% of the vault on average. Of the 11 on Ethereum, 4 are providers of the live vault, 1 has no provider deployed, and 6 need a new adapter: Fluid fUSDC and Spark Savings sUSDC (28.1% and 28.4% of the vault on average), the Morpho cbBTC and WBTC markets, and two Morpho Vault V2 vaults (table 4 of the figures, column "Thesauros adapter"; table 2 for the shares). "A provider of the live vault" is here what the registry of the Thesauros repository lists for the vault; the provider list was not read from the vault contract.

Every number below is copied from a table that a script of the pack prints from the run files (`figures-base.md`, `figures-ethereum.md`, `figures-sensitivity.md`, `figures-first-programme.md`), from `allocator-check.txt`, or from a file and column named beside it. "Table 9" means table 9 of the figures file of the network. Labels and cells of the tables are copied as printed, so a date inside a cell is year-month-day; pp is percentage points. Differences are taken by the script before rounding. What is said of the live contracts is a chain read of 10 October 2026 at the two blocks above, except their provider lists, which are taken from the registry of the Thesauros repository.

## What a depositor would have earned

The main run on each network is the allocator on set A: folders `base-allocator-set-a` and `ethereum-allocator-set-a`, columns `base.alloc-a` and `ethereum.alloc-a` of the figures. Set A is the venue set of a written rule, decided month by month: USDC venues with no queue, cooldown, epoch or notice period on withdrawal and at least $100M of USDC at the monthly reading, and, for a curated vault or a single Morpho market, at least 90% of assets idle or lent against the BTC and ETH tokens the rule lists. Venues run by protocol governance are admitted by name without that collateral test: Aave V3 on Base (2.1% of the main run's interest), and Aave V3, Compound V3, Fluid fUSDC and Spark Savings sUSDC on Ethereum (9.7%, 4.3%, 31.1% and 27.9%). The rule was written after the window closed, by people who knew the results of an earlier version of this pack (see The venue rule). The choice of set A as the main set was made with the same knowledge: the programme of 10 October 2026 named it after the first programme had run set A and the strict set under the public vault's rules. After the cap fee, against Just Aave, that had given +$38,558 on set A and +$3,023 on the strict set on Base, and −$47,213 and −$47,936 on Ethereum (`figures-first-programme.md`, table 1; see Earlier versions).

Names. Thesauros Simple (`thesauros_simple` in the files) is the vault under the decision rule of the run, paying the performance fee. The four controls pay no Thesauros fee. Just Aave (`just_aave`) is the whole deposit in Aave V3, never moved. Equal parts (`eligible_equal_weight`, "Eligible equal weight" in the tables) is the deposit in equal parts over the venues the set allows; it follows the set as the months change. First split held (`first_split_held`) is the run's first allocation, never rebalanced; it keeps a venue after the venue has left the set. Just Best Vault (`just_best_vault`) is the whole deposit in the venue with the highest rate at the first reading, never moved; the liquidity rule binds neither it nor Just Aave.

**How to read the fee rows.** The runs are made at the cap the contract allows: 20% on Base, 25% on Ethereum. The result at another fee is worked out from the run's daily file: each day's return before the fee, less the fee's share on a positive day. At 0% that gives 4.60% and 4.08%. "Thesauros Simple before its fee" in table 1 is $458,213 (4.58%) and $406,047 (4.06%): it adds the year's fees back to the yield, without what they would have earned had they stayed in the vault. This readme takes what a depositor holds at a fee from table 9, and takes the comparisons with a control before the fee from table 1, the only place the figures print them.

**Against Aave.** On Base the depositor is ahead of Just Aave at every fee in the table: by $133,407 at 0%, $86,444 at 10%, $63,041 at 15% and $39,690 at the cap of 20%. The two are equal at a fee of 28.5%, above the cap. That lead was made with 96.2% of the vault in one Morpho market on average, held directly and through vaults that lend into it (table 6); the 60% limit counts venues and does not count that. On the strict set, which has that market once, the lead on Base is $87,294 at 0%, $45,118 at 10%, $24,094 at 15% and $3,113 at the cap, and the two are equal at a fee of 20.7% (table 9, column `base.alloc-strict`). On Ethereum the depositor is ahead at 0% ($57,425) and at 10% ($15,880), and behind at 15% ($4,830) and at the cap of 25% ($46,128). The two are equal at a fee of 13.8%. Of Just Aave's $350,663 on Ethereum, $34,352 (9.8%) was credited on 23 days on which Aave V3 could not have paid the position out; of the main run's interest $8,569 (2.1%) fell on such days (table 11).

**Against the same venues with no rebalancer and no fee.** After the cap fee the depositor has less than equal parts and less than the first split held, which pay no fee, on both networks: $47,632 less than equal parts and $70,064 less than the first split held on Base, $63,508 and $44,089 less on Ethereum. A fee of 10% is already enough to put the depositor under equal parts: the depositor's yield is then 4.13% on Base and 3.67% on Ethereum, and equal parts with no fee made 4.14% and 3.68%. The first split held does not follow the rule: it holds a venue outside set A on 212 days on Base and on 181 days on Ethereum (table 2).

**What the allocator adds before the fee.** Before its fee (table 1) the main run made $44,011 (0.44 pp) more than equal parts on Base and $38,004 (0.38 pp) more on Ethereum, and $21,578 and $57,423 more than the first split held. Choosing by rate under either rule gives most of that lead: the public vault's rules on the same set are ahead of the same equal parts by $42,596 (0.43 pp) and $31,685 (0.32 pp), and of their own first split held by $20,118 and $58,362 (column P of All runs, where what the allocator changes against those rules is set out). And on Base the whole deposit left in the Morpho cbBTC market from the first day, with no rebalancer and no fee, made $457,845 (4.58%), where the main run made $458,213 (4.58%) before its fee and $366,571 (3.67%) after it; on Ethereum that control, Just Best Vault, made $328,922 (3.29%), less than Just Aave. Counting months from the 7th to the 6th, the main run before its fee was ahead of equal parts in 12 of 12 on Base and 11 of 12 on Ethereum (table 1); no table counts the months after a fee. Three things stand beside that lead. On Ethereum it rests on two venues: without Fluid fUSDC the lead over equal parts is $19,574, and without Fluid fUSDC and Spark Savings sUSDC it is $5,091, with the run at $357,093 (3.57%) before its fee against Just Aave's $350,663 (3.51%) (`figures-sensitivity.md`, table 1). On Base the venues of set A are largely one market: on the strict set, which leaves out the vaults that lend into that market and has two venues, the lead over equal parts is $11,028 and the run is $1,489 behind its own first split held (see All runs). And interest is credited whether or not the venue could have paid the position out that day: $1,065 (0.2%) of the main run's interest on Base and $8,569 (2.1%) on Ethereum fell on such days, and so did $4,605 (1.4%) and $34,352 (9.8%) of Just Aave's own (table 11).

**The fee as it stands.** `getPerformanceFee()` read 0 on both live vaults at the blocks named at the top, and it has been 0 since deployment. The cap is what the contract allows, not a fee that has been set, and neither the fee nor the cap is fixed. The admin role, a 2-of-2 Safe on Base and one externally owned account on Ethereum, can set the performance fee up to the cap in one transaction, with no timelock. The contract also allows a management fee of up to 5% of assets a year, charged whether or not the vault earns; it read 0 on both vaults and no run charges it. On the older Base vault of the same name both fees are set, a 20% performance fee and a 1.5% management fee (chain read of 10 October 2026); no table of this pack shows a result with a management fee. The account that owns the proxy admin can replace the implementation, and with it the cap. How a fee would be divided between Thesauros and Tangem is not set in this pack (see Fees).

**What the year allowed.** With none of our money in it, the best venue of set A grew by 4.73% over the year on Base (the Morpho cbBTC market) and by 4.29% on Ethereum (Fluid fUSDC); Aave V3 grew by 3.55% and 3.58% (table 4, index growth). The main run made 4.58% and 4.06% before its fee, under what the best single venue paid with none of our money in it on both networks. With $10M of ours in it the best venue's mean rate is 4.61% on Base (the Morpho cbBTC market) and 3.81% on Ethereum (Spark Savings sUSDC; Fluid fUSDC 3.59%) (table 4, "With $10M of ours"; a mean of rates read back, not a yield): the Base run is under it and the Ethereum run above it. Just Aave made 3.27% and 3.51% with the deposit's own effect on Aave's rate counted, and 3.55% and 3.58% in the runs in which the deposit moves no rate (`figures-sensitivity.md`, table 1). The level of every number here is what these venues paid in this one year. The differences against equal parts and the first split held are tenths of a percentage point. Against Just Aave the depositor on Base is +1.33 pp at a fee of 0%, +0.86 pp at 10%, +0.63 pp at 15% and +0.40 pp at the cap, a lead made with 96.2% of the vault in one Morpho market; on Ethereum +0.57 pp at 0% and −0.46 pp at the cap (table 9).

**Venues held.** A venue counts as held on a day when it has 1% of the vault or more at the day's close. On Base the main run held one venue on 1 day, two on 143, three on 209 and four on 12: 2.64 on average. On Ethereum it held one venue on 19 days, two on 117, three on 78, four on 115, five on 34 and six on 2: 3.09 on average. On the days with one venue that venue is Aave V3 (table 10).

**Base: one book.** Set A on Base is Aave V3, the Morpho cbBTC market and five Morpho vaults that held 86.14% to 99.99% of their assets in that market at the monthly readings at which they are in the set (table 5). The main run had 96.2% of the vault in that one market on average, 52.3% directly and 43.9% through vaults, and 100.0% at the close of 19 October 2025 (table 6). That market lends against one token, cbBTC, at an 86% liquidation loan-to-value, and its oracle prices cbBTC with a BTC/USD feed alone, with no feed for cbBTC itself (see the last table of The venue rule): most of the Base result stands behind one collateral token and one oracle. The limit of 60% of the vault in a venue counts venues, not what stands behind them, so it does not bound this exposure. The stress scenarios hit one venue: the total-loss scenario on Base takes the position in the Morpho cbBTC market, $6,115,518 (59.99%), and leaves whole the vaults that lend into the same market (table 3; see Stress scenarios). The comparison to read beside the main run is the strict set, which leaves those vaults out: two venues, Aave V3 and the market, 58.9% of the vault in the market on average. There the allocator made $412,491 (4.12%) before its fee, and after the cap fee the depositor is $3,113 (0.03 pp) ahead of Just Aave, against $39,690 on set A.

**Base: the vaults are priced as separate venues.** The simulation measures the vault's effect on a rate venue by venue. A vault and the market it lends into move each other's rate; that is not counted, so the runs on set A credit a little too much. The pack lets a reader size it. Just Best Vault is on Base the whole $10M in the Morpho cbBTC market itself all year, with the full effect of $10M on that market measured and no fee: it made $457,845 (4.58%). The main run made $458,213 (4.58%) before its fee (table 1). The strict-set run has no such overlap on Base.

## Set B: any collateral

Set B is tests 1 to 3 of the rule (USDC, withdrawal on demand, at least $100M) with no test of what a venue lends against. It adds three venues to set A on Base and twelve simulated venues on Ethereum.

**Base: the same run.** `base.alloc-b` and `base.alloc-a` are one run of `thesauros_simple`: the same balance and fee on every day and the same movements (note under table 1 of `figures-base.md`). The three added venues, Steakhouse High Yield USDC V2, the Morpho USDe market and the Morpho HERMES market, are at 0.00% of the vault on every day. No plan into them was proposed at two readings in a row, and the HERMES market is in set B on a size that is interest accrued on a market lent out in full, so the liquidity rule kept the run out of it. One figure differs, a control: equal parts over set B made $408,914 (4.09%), against $414,203 (4.14%) over set A.

**Ethereum: a loss.** The run made $266,204 (2.66%) before its fee and $146,319 (1.46%) after it. That is $204,344 less than Just Aave, and $82,120 less at a fee of 0%: no fee brings it level (tables 1 and 9). The cause is one venue on one day. At the day closes of 6 to 10 November 2025 the vault held 59.99% of its balance in Usual Boosted USDC, a Morpho vault with 0.0000% of its assets against the rule's listed collateral at the reading of 7 October 2025 (table 5). Between the readings of 14:23 and 18:25 UTC on 11 November 2025 the venue's index fell by 3.56% (`UsualBoostedMorphoProvider.index` of `reproduce/data/tangem/ethereum/raw.jsonl`). The venue's interest for that day is −$213,753 and the vault's yield for the day −$213,335 (`interest_usual_boosted_usdc` and `daily_yield` of `ethereum-allocator-set-b/simulation/tangem-simulation-daily.csv`); over the year the venue's interest in the run is −$194,622 (table 7). The position left at 22:36 UTC on 12 November 2025, 28 hours after the reading that shows the fall, and not because of the loss: the movement is a forced exit, the venue having become too small for a full position (`reason` of the rebalances file). That size limit is the simulation's, not production's, and no rule of the allocator makes the vault leave a venue after a loss. The run with measured exponents did the same and made $149,802 (1.50%) after its fee.

**Why the vault was there.** The allocator has no rule that reads what a venue lends against, and none that reads a loss. The liquidity rule did not keep it out either: that rule delays an entry after a squeeze and does not prevent it. A venue's signal is zero only at the readings at which its free liquidity is below a full position, 60% of the vault. The rates of those readings stay in its 12-hour average, so once liquidity is back the venue looks attractive and the vault enters at the next two readings. Usual Boosted USDC had less than a full position free at three readings of 4 November 2025, at rates of 36% to 39% (`UsualBoostedMorphoProvider.liquidity` and `.rate` of the same `raw.jsonl`). The vault entered at 01:15 UTC on 5 November 2025 and filled the position the next day; it had held $1.87M there once before, from 16:45 UTC on 11 October to 16:53 UTC on 12 October 2025 (rebalances file of the run). At 21:36 UTC on 6 November 2025 the venue had $0 free against $6.04M held (table 8).

**What the simulation allowed.** Four movements of this run, $8,824,250 in all, took more out of a venue than the venue had free without our deposit. They are: $2,086,407 out of the Morpho mHYPER market at 01:15 UTC on 5 November 2025, with nothing free, which is the movement that began the position in Usual Boosted USDC; $2,744,405 out of the Morpho srUSD market at 17:45 UTC on 7 November 2025, with nothing free; $3,993,386, the largest, out of Gauntlet USDC Frontier at 18:52 UTC on 22 March 2026, with $718,401 free, a vault for which lost assets are reported between the readings of 7 March and 7 April 2026 (see Real events in the window); and $52 out of Fluid fUSDC at 16:17 UTC on 29 May 2026, with nothing free (table 11; `amount` and `source_free_liquidity` of the rebalances file). ## What the allocator did

**The rule.** The allocator spreads the vault so that the last dollar earns the same in every venue it uses, counting how the vault's own deposit lowers each venue's rate. Money already in a venue is judged at the venue's current rate, money entering a venue at the lower of the current rate and its 12-hour average. It moves when the same venues are to be filled and drained at two readings in a row, the plan moves at least $1,000, and the expected gain is at least 0.05 pp of the vault a year and, over seven days, more than twice a flat gas cost of $0.10. A decision aims at no more than 10% of a venue and, in these runs, at no more than 60% of the vault in a venue. The table sets the main run beside the public vault's rules on the same set.

**What the allocator does not do.** No rule of the allocator reads a loss in a venue, a freeze or a pause of a venue, or what a venue lends against, and in production free liquidity is not an input of its step. Three things that act in these runs are the simulation's and not production's: the venue set by month, the limit that reads a venue too small for a full position as paying nothing, and the rule of no entry where liquidity is short. The circuit breaker, the anomaly gate, the cooldown and the minimum of two venues belong to the public vault's RiskGuard. They are not on the allocator's path, and the allocator runs apply none of them.

## Fees

**How the contract charges.** Both live vaults run the same `Rebalancer.sol`. Its `_accruedFees` and `_applyFees` take the performance fee as a share of the rise in assets since the last accrual and mint it to the treasury as vault shares. `_applyFees` then sets the reference, `_lastTotalAssets`, to the present assets whether they rose or fell. It runs on every deposit, mint, withdrawal and redemption, when a fee or the treasury is set, and on `applyFees()`, which anyone may call. There is no high-water mark: no field stores a peak, so after a loss the reference is the lower balance and the fee is charged on the recovery. The deployed bytecode of both vaults matches the build records of the contracts repository. The Ethereum source is also verified on Sourcify; for Base no explorer source was read.

**Caps and present values.** The performance fee is capped at 20% on the Base vault and 25% on the Ethereum vault (`MAX_PERFORMANCE_FEE`; the setter reverts above it, shown by simulating the call on 10 October 2026). `getPerformanceFee()` read 0 on both vaults at the blocks named at the top, and the only `PerformanceFeeUpdated` events since deployment are those of the initialisation, with value 0. The contract also allows a management fee of up to 5% of assets a year, charged whether or not the vault earns; `getManagementFee()` read 0 on both. The admin role sets either fee in one transaction, without the timelock: on Base a 2-of-2 Safe holds the role, on Ethereum one externally owned account. Both vaults are upgradeable proxies. On both networks the proxy admin is owned by the same externally owned account, `0xafA9ed53c33bbD8DE300481ce150dB3D35738F9D` (on Base it carries an EIP-7702 delegation), which can replace the implementation, and with it the cap, in one transaction. The same address is the treasury of both vaults, to which the fee shares are minted, the `owner()` of both timelocks and the holder of the admin role on Ethereum. The Safe on Base has two owners, both addresses without code, and its own nonce is 0. What the owner of a timelock can do was not read.

**What the runs charge.** Every run is made at the cap of its network and charges no management fee (`performance_fee_percent` 20 on Base and 25 on Ethereum, `management_fee_percent` 0, `high_water_mark` false in the configs), except the two `alloc-a.fee10` configs of the check below, which have a performance fee of 10. The runs apply the fee once a day, to that day's gain (`fee_basis` of the daily file). In the contract it is applied at every deposit and withdrawal of any holder and whenever anyone calls `applyFees()`, so how often a live vault applies it depends on its flows.

**The fee table.** Table 9 of each figures file has, for every run, the balance and the yield at a fee of 0%, 10%, 15% and the cap, each against Just Aave, and the fee at which the run equals Just Aave. A level other than the cap is worked out from the run's daily file: each day's return before the fee, which is the interest of the day less gas over the balance of the day before, less the fee's share when the day is positive. At the cap the arithmetic is the run itself; for the main runs the balance worked out differs from the run's by −$0.12 on Base and +$0.17 on Ethereum. The arithmetic keeps the run's returns as they are, and a run made at a lower fee has a larger vault and decides on other amounts. To measure that, the main run was made at 10% on each network (`config.<network>.alloc-a.fee10.json`; the pack has the config and no copy of the files). Base: worked out $10,413,324, made $10,413,313, a difference of $11. Ethereum: worked out $10,366,543, made $10,366,479, a difference of $64.

**Where the missing high-water mark shows.** Twelve of the fourteen runs of the pack have no day with a negative yield, so in them the missing high-water mark changes no figure (`daily_yield` of `thesauros_simple` in each `tangem-simulation-daily.csv`). The two set B runs on Ethereum have one such day each, 11 November 2025. There the fee was $119,885 and $121,054 in years that made $266,204 and $270,856 before it: the fee was taken on each positive day and nothing came back on the day of the loss.

**Not set here.** How a fee would be divided between Thesauros and Tangem is not set in this pack. Every fee figure is the whole performance fee.

## The venue rule

The rule is `venue-rule/VENUE_RULE.md`. Its table is `venue-rule/<network>/venue-eligibility.csv`: one row for each of 70 candidates, 22 on Base and 48 on Ethereum, in each of twelve months, with the result of every test. A venue is in a set for a month if it passes at the first reading of that month, which is the collector's first reading at or after 00:00 UTC on the 7th, from 7 October 2025 to 7 September 2026. On Ethereum that reading falls up to 2 hours 41 minutes after midnight. No rate, yield or outcome of the year enters a test. The tests, as the last commit of the file applies them:

1. **Asset.** USDC on Ethereum or Base, deposited and withdrawn as USDC, one transaction each, at the venue's own contract. A venue that refuses a deposit from an address it has not onboarded fails.
2. **Withdrawal on demand.** No queue, cooldown, epoch or notice period.
3. **Size.** At least $100M of USDC supplied, read on-chain.
4. **Collateral, for a curated vault and for a single Morpho market.** At least 90% of its assets are idle USDC or lent against WBTC, cbBTC, tBTC, WETH, wstETH, cbETH or rETH, read on-chain and looked through to the markets.

## Method

**Readings.** The history is one reading of every venue about every four hours: 2,191 on Base, exactly four hours apart, and 2,180 on Ethereum, 3.41 to 4.61 hours apart. The first is at 00:00 UTC on 7 October 2025 (Base block 36502927, Ethereum block 23522163) and the last at 00:00 UTC on 7 October 2026 (blocks 52270927 and 26136783). A run has 365 daily rows, 7 October 2025 to 6 October 2026. A reading has, for each venue, the rate, the size (`supplied`), the free liquidity, an index whose growth is what a deposit earned, and the rate read back after a deposit of $4M, of $6M and of $10M. The collector tries each of those deposits inside one `eth_call` at the block of the reading, through the venue's own contract, and sends nothing. `raw.jsonl` holds every reading the simulation uses.

**Rates with our own deposit.** A lending rate falls as money arrives. A position earns the rate the three probes measured: the measured points joined by straight lines, the last line continued above $10M. Interest is the growth of the venue's index between two readings, scaled by the ratio of the rate with our position to the rate without it, measured at the start of the interval, and never above that growth. A reading that measures no ratio leaves the venue's last one. A fall of the index is a loss and is taken in full.

**The decision.** The allocator runs call `decideWaterFill` of `reproduce/src/policies.ts` at every reading. It is a port of `PlannerService.intraChainRebalance`, the step that the Thesauros cross-chain planner applies inside one network. It uses the production allocation library (`reproduce/src/allocation/`: the water-filling solver, the impact curve, the smoothed signal) and repeats the planner's own steps around it in its own code. The simulator builds its inputs: each venue's rate with the vault's position in it, a signal that is the lower of that rate and its 12-hour exponentially weighted average (half-life 6 hours), the positions, an impact exponent for each venue, the venue's size with our position in it, and the entry provider. The settings are the planner's defaults (10% of a venue, a move of at least $1,000 with legs under $10 dropped, a gain of at least 0.05 pp of the vault a year and over seven days more than twice $0.10, the same venues filled and drained at two decisions in a row) plus the 60% limit, which is not the planner's. The runs under the public vault's rules call `decideNew` of the same file, a port of the public vault's decision path: it runs the same library's signal, split and gain and repeats the RiskGuard steps in its own code. No comparison of `decideNew` with production on the runs of this pack is in the pack.

**The check against production.** Every decision of the Thesauros run without stress of every allocator config was put to the production function, called as it is and offline, with its own state carried from reading to reading, and the two results were compared: whether a move is made, its legs to the unit, the plan's two sets of venues and its count. There are 2,190 decisions in each of the 7 Base configs and 2,179 in each of the 9 Ethereum configs, 34,941 in all; the main runs have 66 and 232 moves among them. None differs (`allocator-check.txt`). A decision to move is not always a rebalance made: for the set B run on Ethereum the file counts 195 decisions to move, and the run has 194 rebalances and 2 legs refused, on 12 November 2025 (table 2; `rebalances_refused` of its daily file). The same file records a check of the liquidity rule: five constructed cases hold, and on 14,904 random inputs with a venue of zero signal, 14,042 of them a move, no leg entered such a venue. What the comparison shows is that the port decides as production does on the inputs the simulator built. It does not show that the simulator builds them as production would: the signal from four-hour readings, the exponents, the sizes. It does not cover the decisions of the four stress scenarios. It does not cover what the simulation has none of: new deposits, which in production leave the entry provider in the same plan without waiting; a venue whose balance cannot be read; limits that differ from venue to venue; and the planner's minimum of 14 minutes between the two decisions, which cannot bind at four-hour readings. The check needs the production repository and cannot be re-run from the pack; its script is `reproduce/src/check-allocator.ts`, for reading.

**Timing.** The deposit lands in Aave V3 and moves at the fifth reading at the earliest, 16 hours later: a rate history of 12 hours is needed, and a plan has to be seen at two readings in a row. Nine of the ten allocator runs of the pack first move at that reading; the set B run on Ethereum first moves at the eighth, at 04:09 UTC on 8 October 2025 (`datetime_utc` of the first `thesauros_simple` row of each rebalances file). A move is made four hours after it is first seen. The planner's step is scheduled every 15 minutes and on certain on-chain events, so a service running it would take the second look at least 14 minutes after the first; by the team's note of 8 October 2026 no production service was running it. What the plan was between two readings is not seen. Production reads a venue's rate at the hour marks of the last 12 hours; here those hourly points are filled in on a straight line between readings four hours apart.

**The venue set, the size limit and the liquidity rule.** These three are the simulation's, not production's. A venue outside the set for the month, one too small for a full position (60% of the vault would be more than 10% of the venue with the position in it), and one with no reading are read as paying nothing: no money enters, and what the vault holds there leaves when a move can execute. The rebalance file calls that a forced exit. Under the liquidity rule a venue whose own free liquidity at a reading is below a full position has a zero signal at that reading, and any leg into it is dropped; what the vault already holds there is judged at the venue's current rate and kept. The rule delays an entry and does not prevent it (see Set B: any collateral and What the allocator did).

**Moves.** A rebalance executes only if every movement can: the source can pay what is taken out, and the destination did not refuse the deposit probe for the whole position it would hold. The simulation takes the movements of a rebalance to be one transaction that is not sent when one of them cannot execute, so one such movement refuses the whole plan; refused plans are counted in `rebalances_refused`. That the contract reverts as a whole in that case was not verified here. What a source can pay is its own free liquidity and our position on top of it: one assumption, that nobody reacts to our deposit, serves the rate and the exit.

**What could be withdrawn.** `available_liquidity` of the daily file follows a stricter reading than the moves. It is the lowest, over the readings of the day and the first reading after its close, of the sum over the venues of the lesser of our position and the venue's own free liquidity, with none of our deposit counted as liquid.

**Controls.** Equal parts splits the deposit equally over the venues that take money at the first reading and splits again on each date on which the set changes; it reads no rate and moves venue by venue. First split held is the run's first allocation, not moved afterwards. Just Aave and Just Best Vault never move. None pays a Thesauros fee. The venue rule binds the Thesauros run and the equal-parts control at every reading, and the first split and Just Best Vault at the start only. The liquidity rule and the limit on our share of a venue bind the first two at every reading, the first split up to the first rebalance, and Just Aave and Just Best Vault not at all.

**Costs.** A movement of funds uses 982,930 gas, the median of the nine real one-movement rebalance transactions of the live Base vault (`reproduce/data/tangem/rebalance-gas.csv`), priced at the median base fee plus the median priority fee of the 300 blocks that end at the reading; a transaction on Base also pays the L1 data fee of one rebalance transaction. Both are converted at the ETH price of the reading and charged to the vault. When it decides, the allocator prices a transaction at a flat $0.10, as production does. The fee is described in Fees.

## Limits

- **A backtest, not a forecast.** One year, one deposit, no deposits or withdrawals after the first day. The level of every result is what these venues paid in this year.
- **One assumption serves the rate and the exit.** Nobody reacts to our deposit: it earns the lower rate the probes measured and can be taken out again on top of the venue's own free liquidity. That is optimistic when other depositors are leaving. No movement of the main runs took out more than the source had free without us; 4 and 3 of the two set B runs on Ethereum and 435 of the Base vault as deployed did (table 11). The runs in which the deposit moves no rate are the other end of this one assumption (`figures-sensitivity.md`); the other assumptions are not bracketed by that pair.
- **Interest is credited whether or not it could have been withdrawn.** Table 11 has the amount for every run and control: $1,065 and $8,569 for the main runs, $37,628 for the set B run on Ethereum, $247,480 for the Base vault as deployed, and $4,605 and $34,352 for Just Aave.
- **Impact is measured venue by venue.** A vault and the market it lends into lower each other's rate, and that is not counted. On Base, where 96.2% of the main run sat in one market, this credits the run a little too much (see Where the yield came from).
- **The 60% limit counts venues, not books, and the entry provider has no limit.** The main run on Base had up to 100.0% of the vault in one market, itself and through vaults (table 6). Aave V3, the entry provider, was the only venue held, at 100.00% of the vault, at the close of 1 day on Base and of 19 days on Ethereum (table 10), and held more than 60% at the close of 8 and of 23 (`pct_aave_v3` of the daily files).
- **The 10% limit is on what a decision aims at, not on what is held.** A venue that shrinks under a position leaves the vault above it. The largest share reached was 2.6% and 5.7% of a venue in the main runs, and 20.4% in the set B run on Ethereum (table 2).
- **The simulation does not see a pause or a freeze.** It reads a venue's free USDC as payable. During the two pauses of Compound V3 the readings record $61.42M to $78.67M and $113.45M to $125.06M as free when no withdrawal was possible. In the seven Ethereum runs of the pack the Thesauros run held no Compound V3 at any day close of either pause and moved nothing in or out during them, so no figure of `thesauros_simple` rests on it; the yield of the equal-parts control does. The equal-parts control held Compound V3 through both pauses, 7.68% to 19.99% of its balance in those seven runs, and could not have withdrawn it. The freezes of Aave's collateral reserves are not read either; what the readings show of Aave V3 in those days is in Real events in the window.
- **Exponents for venues with no production adapter are an assumption.** Production has a constant for Aave V3, Compound V3 and a first-generation Morpho vault. A Morpho Vault V2, a single Morpho market, Fluid and Spark Savings are given 1.75, and among them are the largest holding of the main run on Base and the two largest on Ethereum. The runs with measured exponents are the comparison; where a probe is steeper than an exponent of 10 gives, they use 10.
- **Four-hour readings.** Decisions are made at each reading. Whether decisions every 15 minutes would change the number of moves, or the count of moves after which the vault's rate was lower, was not tested.
- **Gas.** One gas figure, measured on the Base vault, is used for every venue on both networks, the venues with no adapter included; the Ethereum vault has made no rebalance, and its rows in `rebalance-gas.csv` are estimates. Inside the decision a transaction costs a flat $0.10, a figure production set for L2 networks, while the main run on Ethereum paid $716 of gas for its 232 transactions.
- **What running the main run would take.** The planner's registry has no Ethereum network, and the planner acts on the contracts of the cross-chain stack, not on the two public vaults of this pack. No production service was running it by the team's note of 8 October 2026. It reads each venue's limit from the strategy contract, and those limits were not read. Also not read for this pack: the provider list and the entry provider of each live vault as the contract returns them, whether either vault is paused, its `minAssets`, and what its timelock allows beyond `delay()` and `owner()`. A live vault would also need the adapters named at the top, the provider list changed through the timelock (`setProviders`; the delay is one hour) and the entry provider set by the admin role. The venue set by month, the size limit and the liquidity rule would have to be written: production has none of them.
- **Sizes in the rule.** `size_usd` is the venue's stored total at the reading. For a market lent out in full it is interest accruing on paper: sixteen venue-months of set B are there on such a figure (see Set B: any collateral).
- **Shared exposure and the liquidity figure.** The look-through of table 6 is read once a month and applied to every day of the month. The liquidity figure is venue by venue: the free USDC of a Morpho market counts once for the market and once for each vault that lends into it. On Base the market never had less than $63M free (table 4), more than the whole vault, so nothing is overstated there. On Ethereum that was not checked for these runs.
- **Rates of venues with no Thesauros adapter** are computed by the collector's own lens contract (`reproduce/src/TangemLensWide.sol`). A Morpho Vault V2 and Fluid state no rate of their own, so for them the lens's figure was not compared with a rate the venue reports.
- **Interest is the growth of a venue's index.** Rewards that a venue pays outside its index, if any, are in no figure.
- **Collateral.** The Aave and Compound lists are what could be posted, at two dates; which collateral the borrowed USDC stood against was not read. Fluid's collateral was not read from the chain at all.
- **Stress.** The scenarios are hypothetical and independent, hit one venue on one day and leave whole every other venue that lends into the same market. Their first day, 7 April 2026, is also the first day of a rule month: set A changes that day on Ethereum (the Morpho WBTC market comes back in, Vault Bridge USDC leaves) and not on Base.
- **The controls are not treated alike** by the liquidity rule (see Set B: any collateral), and the first split held keeps venues that later leave the set.

## Verification and reproduction

**Reproduction.** `reproduce/reproduce.sh` runs every simulation again, prints the four figures files again and compares each file with the one in the pack, byte for byte:

```bash
cd reproduce
sh reproduce.sh
```

`./reproduce.sh` does the same where the file has kept its execute bit. It runs `node simulate.js <config>` for each of the 28 configs, compares the four CSV files of each of the fourteen run folders with what it wrote (56 files), then runs `node figures.js` four times and compares the four tables. It ends with the line "Every simulation file and every table reproduces byte for byte." and exit status 0, or names each file that differs. It takes about two minutes and needs `node`, `sh` and `cmp`. It writes `reproduce/data/tangem/<network>/<output>/`, 12 folders on Base and 16 on Ethereum, and `reproduce/data/tangem/figures-*.md`; those do not exist before the first run. A trial build of the pack was reproduced this way before this readme was written, with no file differing. The two bundles are esbuild builds for the target `node18`; the build machine's `node` is v20.18.0, and no run on Node 18 itself is recorded. A single run, made from inside `reproduce/`, is `node simulate.js config.base.alloc-a.json`, which writes `data/tangem/base/simulation-alloc-a/` there: both scripts look for `data/tangem/` under the directory they are started from. The calls of `figures.js` are in `reproduce.sh`. Every parameter of a run, the set by month and the stress scenarios included, is in its config.

**What the reproduction does not cover.** `raw.jsonl`, `venue-eligibility.csv`, `venue-collateral-monthly.csv`, `rebalance-gas.csv` and the two files of `market-data/` are collected from the chain or written by the pack builder; the collector's sources are in `reproduce/src/` for reading, and running them needs an archive node and the Thesauros repository. `allocator-check.txt` needs the production repository. `tangem-simulation.zip` is not compared by the script; it holds copies of three of the four CSV files of its folder.

**Checks inside the two scripts.** `simulate.js` stops unless, for every strategy, the daily yields add up to the final balance and each day's interest less gas and fee is the day's change of the balance, both within a cent, no position is negative, and the rows of the rebalance file equal the daily counts. `figures.js` stops unless the interest of the venues less gas and fee is `daily_yield` on every day, the two gas totals agree within their rounding, the positions it rebuilds for tables 2, 8 and 11 give the share of the daily file within 0.1% of the balance on every day, the balance worked out at the run's own fee is within $1 of the run's, and no plan of the allocator that a gain decided has a movement under $10 or moves less than $1,000 (notes under tables 2, 7, 8, 9 and 11).

**An independent recomputation of the figures.** A second script, written in Python without opening the figures script, recomputed every row that holds a number in tables 1, 2, 9, 10 and 11 of `figures-base.md` and `figures-ethereum.md` from the run files, the configs and `raw.jsonl`: 1,308 cells, compared as text, none differs. Its first version compared 1,182 cells and differs from the present tables in 101, 98 of them in table 11: it weighed a venue's closing position against the venue's lowest liquidity of the day, which finds no day short of liquidity in the main Base run, where the daily file's own `available_liquidity` has two. It was corrected to the rule stated under table 11. Tables 3 and 7 were checked by another script against `tangem-stress-tests.csv` and the daily files: 628 cells, of which 626 agree as text. The other two are sums that end on exactly 50 cents in the files, $27,523.50 and $401,571.50, which table 7 prints as $27,523 and $401,571. These scripts and their output are not in the pack, so the checks cannot be re-run from it.

**The port against production.** `allocator-check.txt`: 34,941 decisions in sixteen runs, none differs. What that covers is in Method.

**The collection.** When the venues of set B were added, the history was collected again. Every value of every venue already in it came out identical, 214,718 values on Base and 303,020 on Ethereum, at all 2,191 and 2,180 readings. The collector's self-checks passed: the five live providers of each network read to the wei as the provider contracts report, and 19 readings on Base and 44 on Ethereum of the venues with no adapter equal plain reads of the venue. The logs of these checks are not in the pack, and they cannot be re-run from it.

**Not repeated for this pack.** Two checks reported with the earlier pack, real deposits on a fork against the deposit probes and monthly mean rates against index growth, were not repeated for the venues added since. Their script is `reproduce/src/verify-on-fork.ts`, for reading; no output of them is in the pack, and no figure from them is quoted here.

**The chain reads.** The facts read from the chain on 10 October 2026 (the live vaults, the fee caps and roles, the two pauses, the collateral lists, the freezes) are given with their blocks and functions, so that they can be read again from an archive node. The two vaults are the proxies named at the top, and the account that owns their proxy admins is named in Fees; the Comet of the two pauses is `0xc3d688B66703497DAA19211EEdff47f25384cdc3`; the Aave pools and the other venues are in table 4 of the figures, column "Contract". The scripts and outputs of those reads are not in the pack.
